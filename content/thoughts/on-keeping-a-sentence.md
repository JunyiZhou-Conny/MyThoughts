This is a sample of form, not a finished claim about a life. Replace it when you have a sentence that is yours.

A spoken note is almost never a paragraph. It circles. It names a feeling twice and loses the verb. It is useful the way a sketch is useful: it proves that something passed through you, and it leaves the shape unfinished.

I do not distrust that first pass. I distrust the hope that the first pass is enough. There is a difference between capturing a thought and keeping one. Capture is speed. Keeping is the slower work of choosing words that can survive being read tomorrow, by a stranger, and still feel true.

The internet is now fluent in a kind of prose that has never been tired, never been unsure, never been rewritten at a kitchen table. I have no quarrel with tools. I have a quarrel with disappearing inside them. If a sentence is going to carry my name, I want to remember the hour I spent making it smaller.

So the method is plain. Speak when the thought arrives — on a train, between sets, walking home. Then sit down and betray the transcript. Cut the extra clauses. Keep the one image that still has blood in it. Do not ask a model to make it elegant. Elegance that you cannot point to in your own ear is only costume.

A kept sentence is not denser information. It is a thought that has agreed to be met.
